How native code's registers are now

  • The stencils (src/native/stencils.cpp) are functions of the calling convention preserve_none, which passes up to twelve whole numbers in the processor's registers, and eight XMM registers. All twelve are taken: the frame's slots, the region's context, nine registers for the region's whole numbers (i0 to i8) and its flag (i9, a comparison's answer). The processor has sixteen: of the four left, RSP is the stack, RBP the convention keeps for its caller, RBX and R10 are free for a stencil's own use.
  • A register kept past the twelve works (tried 2026-11-17, Clang 19): a variable bound to RBX by an empty asm at a stencil's start and again before its jump on keeps its value through the stencil and the jump, and Clang takes another register for its own work.
  • Stencils by registers: 43,165 now. The whole numbers' take 7,938 (2,187 of three registers: add, subtract, multiply, 9 × 9 × 9 each); the floats' the rest (6,144 each for four operations). Eleven registers for whole numbers would add some 4,500, twelve some 7,000.
  • Spilling (src/native/regions.cpp, giveRegisters and spillOne): Chaitin's colouring as Briggs has it; where there are too many values, one is kept in its slot everywhere (each read loaded first, each write stored), chosen by how many it clashes with for how often it is written in the code. Not by how often it runs: no region knows that. A guess at it was tried and dropped (2026-11-17): it served the loops it was made on and cost the blur kernel 13%.
  • A region made again already happens, once, where its quick ways miss too often (vm.cpp): the way the counts of part B would come back.

Part A's spike: RBX and R10 unsound (2026-11-17)

Built as planned: the flag renamed, RBX and R10 bound in every stencil by an empty asm as it starts and as it goes on, eleven registers given out. 48,107 stencils (+4,942), ww.exe 0.73 MB larger; the object file then past COFF's 65,279 sections, so the stencil tool now reads COFF's big form too (kept). The native check passed; the LZW loop was no faster (14.9 ms: its spills still chosen by where values are written, the range loop's own slots among them).

Then the kernels: blur failed (an index out of range) and idct took 662 ms to 398. Two causes, both in what Clang makes of the stencils:

  • The binding is not a reservation. A register variable is in its register only at the asm that names it; Clang may move other work past that asm and use the register for it. In rcntai_0_1 it worked out the range's count after the hand-on asm, in RBX and R10, so register 10 came to the next stencil holding the count. Ten registers (RBX only) gave the right blur by luck: nothing makes it hold. Clang has no way to reserve RBX on x86 (-ffixed-rbx is refused).
  • No register left for Clang's own work. With twelve registers passed and two more bound, a stencil needing one more pushes RBP and pops it again: in most stencils, each step a push and a pop more.

Undone; nothing of it is in the build but the tool's reading of the big form. A sound way to one more register, if wanted: the flag's register (RAX, the twelfth passed) given out as an ordinary register wherever no comparison's answer is in it, the allocator told where the flag lives (each comparison's step writing it, each jump or and reading it); stencils for register 9 that name the flag's. Ten registers, no binding.

Part B, built: counts of what runs (2026-11-17)

At the user's word ("go ahead with B"), in 0.9.5008, after native code came to be made on another core (background-plan.html), which it uses:

  • A loop's region made first counts (regions.cpp's make: Region::counting): a count step as each instruction starts, into counts kept with its code (RegionCounts, bytecode.h); each inner loop's end counting its times round (loopbackn), and the region's own loop's end (loopbackc) leaving for the VM once, past 1,000 times round in all, so that what it counted is a whole time round of its own at least. A whole function's region counts nothing. WW_NATIVE_COUNTS=0: none counts (the code as before).
  • Made again from the counts (VM::remakeCounted): as it leaves so, from the frame there, its counts given with what is taken (NativeSeen::runs), in place or by the builder as the first was. The region made first runs on meanwhile; as the new one is put in, its ways in take the place of the old one's, and the old one's word beside its count is cleared, so that where it is running on (a long loop) it leaves at its next time round and the VM goes on in the new one.
  • What is kept in memory, chosen from the counts (spillOne, spillHome): each use of a value weighed by how often it runs a time round; a write eight times a read (a value written in a loop and kept in memory is stored and loaded again on the way each time round waits for; one only read is loaded beside the work); against a thousandth, not 1 (which drowned the counts: an outer loop's value weighed as much as an inner one's).

Three faults found on the way, each fixed: the region made again from the way in it last came in by (after a call), where no loop starts, so made none, and counted on all the while (the GIF spike's indices 10.7 ms to 18.6); asked for at any way out, a call's too, before its time round was through (what came after the call counted as never run: dithered's inner loop's values kept in memory, 84 k loads to 36 M); the weights above, found on the gaussian kernel (6% slower at a write weighed as a read and 4 times; 8, 16 and 32 alike).

Questions for the user

Each with the recommendation and why.

1How many registers: eleven, or twelve with RBP?

  • Eleven (RBX and R10): nothing to save or put back.
  • Twelve (RBP too): saved as a region is entered and put back on every way out (a step each); a crash inside a region would show no frame chain past it.

Recommended: eleven first, twelve tried in the same spike and kept only if it is measurably quicker on the loops above.

Decided2026-11-17, the user ("go with the recommendations"): eleven first, twelve tried in the spike.

2The order: A, B, C?

  • A, B, C: more registers first (mechanical, helps every loop at once); then counts; then splitting, which needs the counts to tell a hot path from a cold one.
  • A, then C on guesses: splitting without counts, the hot path guessed.

Recommended: A, B, C. Guessing which path is hot is what failed on 2026-11-17; with counts, splitting and spilling both choose from what really runs.

Decided2026-11-17, the user: A, B, C.

3How long a region counts before it is made again

  • The first 1,000 times round (or the loop's first run, if shorter): made again as the loop next starts.
  • The first 10,000 times round: surer counts, longer at the counting region's speed.

Recommended: 1,000: a loop worth native code runs far more than that, and a branch taken once in a thousand matters little to what is spilled.

Decided2026-11-17, the user: the first 1,000 times round.
