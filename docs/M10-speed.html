Speed: Jade beside C++ for M9 and M10

The table M10's plan keeps beside it (part zero, question 14): the work that might be C++ for speed, timed in C++ and in Jade, and what the user decides for each piece at the end. Begun 2026-11-08, when part zero was done; a row is added for each piece as parts 1 to 8 build it, and the timings are taken again at the end.

How they are timed. bench/kernels holds each kernel twice, kernels.jade and kernels.cpp, the same work done the same way, a loop over the items as a program would write it; build.bat builds the C++ with clang-cl at /O2, and time.ps1 runs each kernel on each program, best of three, kept on the fast cores (an i9-14900KF held at 5 GHz). Jade is the Clang build. Every kernel gives the same answer in each, which the script checks. Times in milliseconds.

What the numbers say. A loop in Jade over bytes or pixels is now 8 to 240 times as long as the same loop in C++: least where the work is a few steps on each item (line ends, Huffman decoding, the flood fill, the box blur's running sums), most where it is many small steps of arithmetic on floats (the DCT, the Gaussian blur) or very little work on many items that C++ does in vector registers (shrinking, the gradients). An interpreter cannot close that; the whole-list, grid and picture operations of questions 15 to 17 move the loop itself into C++, which is why that column is expected near C++'s where an operation fits.

What part zero changed in the VM

  • 2a6d4d2: a buffer's byte read and written at once; toByte(), toByteWrapped() and a byte's toInt() as one instruction, with a float's toInt() and toByte() in its quick part and Math's min, max, abs, floor, ceil, round and sqrt just behind; a byte taken as an int by the quick paths and by the checker's typed instructions; int / and %, and an int with a float, worked out at once in register code; an item of a packed List<int> or List<float> read straight from its block. The standard benchmarks: particles 15% faster, none slower.
  • 5c6f235: a small function put in place of its calls, still a call of its own in a stack trace (the user's choice, 2026-11-08). The standard benchmarks: none slower.
